## WORK

**A canon against divination and its concealment - church-order legislation,
NOT Qalementos IV.** Evidence from the ink, strongest first:

1. **Structure.** ll.1-15 are one enumerating litany of sixteen limbs, each
   introduced by ወእመሂ፡በ… / ውስተ… ("or if by … / in …"), closed at l.15 by
   ወበዝንቱ፡ኵሉ "and by all of these", then the agent noun እለ፡ያስግሉ "those who
   divine" (ሰገለ, the standard Ge'ez verb for augury/sorcery; it recurs at l.7).
   The limbs are the classic media of divination: fire (l.1, pyromancy),
   stumbling of the foot (ll.1-2 ዕቅፍተ፡እግር, an omen of the road), the
   turning to right and to left (ll.5-6, augury by the side an omen appears
   on), trees and stones (l.8), hours, day and nights (ll.9-10, hemerology),
   the light of the sun and the light of the moon (ll.10-12), the book (l.12),
   the shadow (l.13, ጽላሎት), the measure of the foot and the measure of the
   hand (ll.14-15). This is a divination catalogue, not a sin-list of the
   "in word, in deed, in thought" absolution type.
2. **Penalty clause with a clerical rank list.** ll.16-24: those who do these
   things, if their deeds done "among them" are exposed and they do not
   confess (ወኢያይድኡ) "neither to the bishop (ኢጲስ፡ቆጶስ) nor to the priest
   (ቀሲስ) nor to the deacon (ዲያቆን)", God will avenge (ይትቤቀሎሙ) and it will be
   required of/by the appointed officers of the church (ስዩማነ፡ቤተ፡ክርስቲያን).
   The rank list runs bishop > priest > deacon, top-down, THREE ranks only -
   no subdeacon, no reader. Compare f.174r, whose list includes subdeacon and
   reader: this page's three-grade list is a different (or a lower-resolution)
   ordering, and sits with the canon-collection stretch ff.176r-177v rather
   than with ff.168-174.
3. **No first-person "we command", no source-citation formula, no proper name,
   no scriptural citation** on this side. The legislation is third-person
   apodictic. So no clause here quotes "the Canons of the Apostles" as
   f.176r-177v did; it is a canon in the same collection, not a citing canon.
4. **Genre continuity with the ff.176r-177v canons** (who may not enter the
   sanctuary; church-order rulings) - and with the penitential canons of
   ff.160v-164r, where the key already records the unplaced divinatory term
   ጋልዮ/ግልዮ. This side is the divination canon those penitential canons
   presuppose.

Against Qalementos IV: the Ethiopic Qalementos is narrative/discursive
Clement-to-Peter revelation; a bare ወእመሂ-litany of augury media closed by a
three-rank confession clause is canon-collection prose. Nothing here is
addressed by Peter to Clement, nothing is in the first person.

Candidate identification within the canon literature: a sorcery canon of the
Sinodos type (the Ethiopic church-order collection carries canons against
ሰገል/ሥራይ with confession to the clergy). I cannot tie it to a numbered canon
from the ink alone; the diagnostic for the expert is the exact sequence
fire - stumbling - [ll.2-4 unresolved] - right/left - [l.6 unresolved] -
trees/stones - hours/day/nights - sun - moon - book - shadow - foot-measure -
hand-measure, which no other agent's page shows and which can be matched
against a printed Sinodos.

## GIST

Whoever practises divination by fire, by a stumble of the foot, by [two
unresolved media], by omens to the right and left, by [unresolved], by trees
and stones, by hours, days and nights, by the light of sun or moon, by the
book, by the shadow, or by measuring foot and hand - all such diviners and
[ያጠብዑ, unresolved] - if their deeds are exposed and they confess neither to
bishop nor priest nor deacon, God will take vengeance on them and it will be
required of/by the appointed officers of the church, and they shall not
[ወኢይመጥ… continues on 180r].

  ቍርባን or the sanctuary, i.e. a ban on admitting the unconfessed diviner to

  another ርኩስ፡ወሰዑብ or ውጉዝ formula against sorcerers, possibly naming ሥራይ or
  the key's unplaced ጋልዮ. A first-person "we command" would not falsify the
