## WORK

Not the Didascalia / Apostolic Constitutions II. This page has left church
order behind: it is a paraenesis on almsgiving that pays out in a paradise
topography. Hypothesis: a homily drawing on the **Apocalypse (Vision) of
Paul**, chs. 21–23, or an Ethiopic derivative of it (the Apocalypse of the
Virgin family reuses the same block). Evidence from the ink:

- ll.18–19 name the four rivers in the order **ኤፌስን፡ኤፍራጥስ፡ግዮን፡ጤግሮስ**
  (Pishon, Euphrates, Gihon, Tigris). That is not the Genesis 2:11–14 order
  (Pishon, Gihon, Tigris, Euphrates); it is the order of the Apocalypse of
  Paul 23, where the four rivers of the city of Christ are Pishon (honey),
  Euphrates (milk), Gihon (oil), Tigris (wine). Nothing else I know puts
  Euphrates second.
- l.17 ገነተ፡ማኅደረ፡ጻድቃን "the garden, dwelling of the righteous" and ll.21–22
  ዘእስተዳለወ፡ለጻድቃን፡ወለስማዕት "prepared for the righteous and the martyrs" is
  the land-of-promise / city-of-Christ frame of Apoc. Paul 21–22, where the
  righteous and the martyrs are shown their prepared places.
- ll.8, 12 ፍሬያት፡ገነት, ሠናይት፡ፍሬያተ፡ጥዑማት "fruits of the garden, good sweet
  fruits" matches the fruit-bearing trees of Apoc. Paul 22.
- ll.15–16 ይበርህ፡ምስብዒተ፡እምፀሓይ "he shines sevenfold more than the sun" is a
  stock apocalyptic reward line (2 Enoch 66:7; Apoc. Paul 21 has the light of
  that land "seven times brighter"). It does not pin the source on its own.
- The page opens (ll.1–5) with the beneficiary: the one who gives alms and
  mercy "with a broad heart" to [the poor?], [widows?] and orphans. Apoc.
  Paul's rewards are also handed out by category of good work.

Held as hypothesis. An Ethiopic Apocalypse of Paul as a standalone text is
not something I can vouch for; a homily quoting it, or a Marian apocalypse
built on it, would both explain the ink. The reverse-order scripture habit
seen on ff.167v and 173v has no test here: no scripture is quoted verbatim on
this page, only the river list, and that list is itself in a non-Genesis order.

Citations on this page, in order of appearance: none verbatim. The river
catalogue (Gen 2:11–14 material) appears in the order Pishon, Euphrates, Gihon,
Tigris.

## GIST

Joy is promised to the man who gives alms and mercy generously to the poor,
widows and orphans; [they] receive him as their companion, love him, and he
rejoices with great joy while [they] show him the good sweet fruits of the
garden, each with its own beauty, and waters of light. If a man is washed in
them he shines sevenfold more than the sun, and [they] bring him to the
garden, the dwelling of the righteous, with Pishon, Euphrates, Gihon and
Tigris and all the rivers in heaven and on earth, and all that is prepared
for the righteous, the martyrs and the good, the work of God, Lord of all,
whose is wisdom.

## NOTES

**The ዎ identification (ll.6, 10, 12, 17).** Four times a glyph occurs that
reads at 2x as ቆ: a single round loop, tall stem, ring at top right. All four
sit where the sense wants a 3mp verb + 3ms object suffix (-ዎ): ወይራስ/ይዎ
(= ይሬስይዎ "they make him"), ያስተፈሥሕዎ "they gladden him", ያርእየዎ (for
ያርእይዎ, "they show him"), ወያዓጽዎ. The key already records that this scribe
draws line-initial ው "like a printed ቍ (single loop, tall stem, top-right
hook)", i.e. the ወ family in this hand can be one loop plus a stem. A ring on
that stem is ዎ. I have transcribed ዎ on that basis and flag it as an
identification, not an ink-certain reading; ቆ is the alternative at every
site and yields no word at any of them. Add to the key if the next agent
confirms it on a neighbouring page.
