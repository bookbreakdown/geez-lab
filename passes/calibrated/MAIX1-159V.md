## GIST

The page opens mid-sentence with "...in his kingdom", then says that those who do not carry out their ministry well will find their seat in the ends of darkness with the devil and his demons, who [lead] men [astray] in their wickedness, and with them they will dwell for ever. The righteous, by contrast, will dwell in eternal life, where there is no sickness or pain, in the bosom of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Peter then turns to Clement: "And thus I command you, O my son Clement, that you rebuke those who shepherd the flock of Christ, so that they command the peoples and [stir/burn?] them with this word." What follows is a run of anathemas in the stock form "accursed and excommunicate is he who...": one against the man who sets up [some divinatory object, ጋልዮ] and an idol in his house and [worships/consults?] them, ...for the work of hands, for men; and one against the man who takes omens from birds and practises ግልዮ and [something] among the trees and [stones?] — the last word is cut by the page end.

## NOTES

Least certain readings, in order of doubt:

- l.1 ስማስቱ and l.1-2 እማያኮ|ቱ. The ink is clear but I cannot make either into a word I know. The first might be the scribe's ሰማዕቱ with the sadis-for-other-order habit, but the third glyph is a plain ስ, not ዕ. The second could be a name. Both are carried over from 159r, so the join check with that folio should settle them.
- l.2 እለሉ: three glyphs, clearly እ ለ ሉ. Possibly a dittography for እሉ or a nonstandard እለ. Left as ink.
- l.5 ወሬእጋንንቲሁ: sense demands ወአጋንንቲሁ "and his demons". Between ወ and the alef there is a small extra glyph, ሬ or ር. It may be a false start the scribe did not erase. Transcribed as ink; second choice: ወር.
- l.5-6 ይ|ሔቱ: sense wants a verb meaning "lead astray" (ያስሕቱ), but there is no ስ on the page. The first glyph is the ይ/ደ collision, chosen as ይ on sense. The ሔ may be ሕ with a sadis mark that looks like a ring.
- l.9-10 ደ|ዊ: for ደዌ "sickness" (ኀበ አልቦ ደዌ ወሐማም). The glyph at the end of l.9 is the ደ/ይ collision, possibly ዲ; chosen on sense.
- l.10 ሕጽኑ: construct ሕፅነ expected ("in the bosom of Abraham"). The last glyph carries a foot hook, so ኑ, not ነ. The ጽ has the bar, not the rounded ፅ bowl.
- l.15 ዔቱ: መርዔተ expected. The glyph carries the foot hook that this scribe uses for ቱ. Left as ቱ.
- l.15-16 የእ|ዝዘሙ: sense wants ያእዝዞሙ "that he/they command them". The ya is unhooked, so የ; the third glyph of l.16 reads ሙ, not ዙ.
- l.17 ህዎሙ (ወያፈልህዎሙ): the glyph is ህ, not ጥ, so not ያፈልጥዎሙ "separate them". Perhaps from ፈልሐ "boil/burn", i.e. "inflame them with this word". Low confidence on the sense; the ink is clear.
- l.17 ቃለተ ። ርጉ|ም: the glyph after ቃለ reads ተ (or ት); the stop after it is two spaced pairs, i.e. ። per the key. An alternative segmentation is ቃል ፡ ትርጉም "the word of interpretation" with the stop read as a single ፡, but that ignores the key's rule on ።. I have kept ቃለተ ። ርጉም. Second choice: ቃል ፡ ትርጉም ፡.
- l.17-18 and l.21-22 ርጉ|ም ፡ ወውጉዝ ፡ ውእቱ ፡ ዘ...: read as the stock anathema formula "accursed and excommunicate is he who". This is the reading that makes the ጉ glyph (see letterform note below) consistent in all four places; the alternative ርት/ውቱዝ gives no words at all.
- l.18-19 ዘይኖ|ብር: the last glyph of l.18 is a ነ- or ገ-family letter with a ring, ኖ or ጎ. Sense suggests ዘያነብር "who sets up" (an idol in his house) but the ya is unhooked and the ring is there. Left as ink.
- l.19 ጋልዮ and l.23 ግልዮ: the same divinatory practice written twice with different first-order vowels (ጋ then ግ). Not a word I can identify; likely an Arabic loan, since Qalementos was translated from Arabic. Transcribed as ink both times.
- l.19 ወጠዓቱ: probably ጣዖት "idol" (ወጣዖተ ውስተ ቤቱ, "and an idol in his house"). The ጠ shows no ጣ leg extension and the second glyph is the 9-shaped ayn without a clear ring, so ዓ rather than ዖ. Second choice: ወጣዖቱ.
- l.20 ይስዮም ፡ ወይስጋዩ: both first glyphs are the ይ/ደ collision, chosen as ይ because both are verbs after a ዘ-clause. Neither verb identified. ጋ in the second is the 7-shaped ge, not the 9-shaped ayn.
- l.20-21 ስ|ቱ: unresolved; the ስ may be ሰ.
- l.21 ለግብሪ ፡ እደ ፡ ለስብእ: read as "for the work of hand(s), for men" (ግብረ እድ ለሰብእ). ሪ has the tick that distinguishes it from ረ on this page. The alef in ስብእ may be the curled ኢ form; I could not settle it and defaulted to the hooked እ.
- l.24 ይለጋዩ: first glyph is the collision, chosen as ይ (a verb after ወ). Verb unidentified. Compare ጋልዮ/ግልዮ above; the same root may be involved.
- l.24 እዕዋም ፡ ወእቋ: "among the trees and..." (አዕዋም). The expected continuation is ወአዕባን "and stones", but the last glyph on the page is a ቀ-family letter with a ring, ቋ or ቆ, not ዕ. Left as ink; the join with 160r will tell.

Letterform facts about this hand worth adding to the key:

- **ጉ is drawn as a ተ with a right-foot hook, and ጉ/ግ at word end can look like ት.** The ge top bar is short and the stem sits nearly central, so it reads as te. On this page it produced ርት for ርጉ and ውቱዝ for ውጉዝ until the anathema formula ርጉም ወውጉዝ ውእቱ forced the correction. Where ተ/ቱ/ት gives no word, test ገ/ጉ/ግ.
- **ቱ's hook is small and sits at the very foot of the right leg**, curling right. It can be mistaken for ቲ. ክ in ክርስቶስ has the same foot kink, which is standard for ክ; do not read it as ኩ.
- **ረ and ሪ are distinct here**: ሪ carries a short tick at the end of the stroke (መንበሪ l.3, ግብሪ l.21); ረ in ይረክቡ (l.3) is plain.
- **The vocative ኦ is written as the plain hooked alef** (እ), with no ring: ኦ ወልድየ appears as እወልድየ (ll.12-13).
- **ዓ and ዖ are both 9-shaped**; the ring that would mark ዖ is often no more than a thickening. Distinguish by sense (በዖፍ l.23 vs ዘለዓለም l.9).
- **ጋ vs ዓ**: ጋ is 7-shaped with the bar to the left; ዓ is 9-shaped. They sit next to each other on l.20 and l.24 and are separable by that alone.
- **Rubrication**: the name ቀሌምንጦስ is written in red mid-line (l.13) without any change of line. The ። that closes the previous sentence is decorated with red dots (l.2). A marginal asterisk-like mark sits beside l.7, at the end of the demons sentence and before the "righteous" sentence; it may be a section or lection mark.
- No numerals appear on this folio, so nothing to add to the numeral table.
