## WORK

Lines 8-14 are Matthew 5:19, quoted verbatim in substance and introduced by
the formula በከመ ፡ ይቤ ፡ በወንጊል "as he said in the Gospel" (ll.7-8). The ink
carries every load-bearing element of the verse: ዘሰ ፡ ፈትሐ "whoever breaks",
ትእዛዛት "commandments", እንተ ፡ ተሐፅፅ "which is least", ሕፁፀ ፡ ይከውን ፡
በመንግሠተ ፡ ሰማያት "shall be least in the kingdom of heaven", ዘሰ ፡ ይሜህር ፡
ወይግብር "but whoever teaches and does", ዐቢየ ፡ ይከውን "shall be great". The
wording differs from the printed Ethiopic Gospel (ይሰመይ "shall be called" is
replaced by ይከውን "shall be"; the order teach/do is reversed from do/teach),
so this is a free citation inside a homily, not a Gospel leaf.

The frame is an address to church leaders: "keep(?) the orders of the holy
church and teach your fellows to do good" (ll.1-4), the Matthew proof-text,
then a catalogue of the good teacher's works (ll.15-24: returns men to God,
turns the sinner to repentance, gives understanding to the fool, looses the
bound, relieves the afflicted, gives drink to the thirsty, strengthens the
broken, raises up...). This continues the apostolic church-order discourse of
ff.168-171. HYPOTHESIS ONLY: Matthew 5:19 applied to the bishop who "does and
teaches" is the argument of Didascalia Apostolorum ch. 2 (= Apostolic
Constitutions II.5-6), and the works-of-mercy catalogue is the Didascalia's
picture of the bishop as physician and shepherd; nothing in the ink names the
work. The catalogue also echoes Isaiah 58:6-7 (loose the bound, feed the
hungry) but no verse is quoted.

## GIST

"...[God?]. And keep(?) the orders of the holy church, and teach your fellows
so that they do good; and let them themselves do it, and they will become great
in the kingdom of heaven, as he said in the Gospel: whoever breaks one of these
commandments which is the least of all, and cuts it off, he too shall be least
in the kingdom of heaven; but whoever teaches and does thus, great shall he be
in the kingdom of heaven. He who teaches men and brings them back to God, and
turns the sinner from sin and returns him to the road of repentance, him who
was not pure; and gives knowledge and understanding to the fool, and looses the
bound, and gives rest(?) to the afflicted, and gives drink to the thirsty, and
strengthens the broken, and raises up..." (continues on 173v).

## NOTES

Uncertain readings, in order of doubt:

- l.1 ወባቁሙ: ink is clear (ወ, box-ባ with left descender, ቀ with bar and a
  foot hook = ቁ, ሙ) but no word results. Context wants "keep/establish the
  orders of the holy church" (ዕቀቡ / አቅሙ). Unplaceable; collect for the expert.
- l.6 ባቢያነ: same box-ባ as l.1. Sense demands ዐቢያነ "great ones" (Matt 5:19
  "great in the kingdom"), and ዐቢየ in l.13 is written with the normal 9-shaped
  ዐ. Two candidate explanations: (a) the scribe has a second, box-shaped ዐ that
  collides with ባ; (b) a genuine ባ spelling. If (a) holds, l.1 becomes ወዐቁሙ,
  still not a word. Transcribed as ink. Final letter written with the up-curling
  foot; read as ነ on morphology (accusative plural), per the widened ነ/ኑ rule.
- l.6 initial ኑ: same foot form, read ኑ on morphology (ይከውኑ 3mp).
- l.3 ቢጸክሙ: the glyph after ቢ was tested against l.8 ዘ (H-shape with top
  tick) and l.23 ጽ at 4x and is the ጸ body with top stem, not the H. Read as
  ጸ. Standard ቢጽክሙ "your fellows"; sadis->ጸ is the scribe's known habit.
- l.10 እልፉ: clear ink, no word. Matthew 5:19 wants እምኵሉ "of all" here. ል is
  the two-legged ል, not ኵ (no top-right hook); ፉ is ፈ with a foot hook, no
  bounding bars. Flag; do not smooth.
- l.11 ቱኪ: ከ-family body with right dash (ኪ). Sense wants ውእቱኒ "he too" or
  ውእቱኬ "he then"; ኬ would need a ring, ኒ a ነ body. Transcribed as ink.
- l.16 ወያግብፅ and l.18 ወያግብፆ: final glyph in both is the ፀ family (looped
  body with foot / with ring), same shape as ፅ in ተሐፅፅ l.9 and ሕፁፀ l.11.
  Expected ወያገብእ / ወያገብዖ "returns (him)". The scribe distinguishes ዕ (l.23
  ጽሙዕ, 9-bowl with top hook) and ዖ (l.24 ጠብዖ), so this is not a ዕ/ፅ
  collision in the hand; it may be a scribal substitution. Transcribed as ink.
  First glyph of l.16 has a short bar at lower left and could be ቀ; read ወ on
  sense.
- l.22 እሱር: second glyph has a flat top and a foot hook; ሱ and ሕ both fit.
  ሕ gives no word; ሱ gives እሱር "the bound one", object of ወይፍታሕ "looses".
  Transcribed ሱ on sense.
- l.22 ወያዐርሞ: penultimate glyph is two bowls plus ring (ሞ), not the ፈ body.
  ያዐርም is not a known verb; ወያዕርፎ "gives him rest" is what the catalogue
  wants. ዐ is the 9-bowl without top hook; ዕ expected. Transcribed as ink.
- l.24 ወያነሠእ: ሠ without visible foot; ሥ expected (ያነሥእ).
- l.3 and l.14 end with a faint ፡; kept.

New letterform facts for the key:
- A box-shaped glyph with a left descender (ባ) appears where sense demands ዐ
  (l.6 ዐቢያነ), on the same page as the ordinary 9-bowl ዐ (l.13). Test for a
  ባ/ዐ collision on other pages before treating either l.1 or l.6 as settled.
- ዕ is distinguishable from ዐ on this page: 9-bowl with a top-right hook
  (l.23 ጽሙዕ) against the plain 9-bowl (l.13, l.22).
- ዖ (l.24) = 9-bowl plus ring; ፆ (l.18) = looped ፀ body plus ring. They are
  separable by the body, not by the ring.
- ሆ (l.5 ለሊሆሙ) carries a small ring at top right, separable from ሁ (l.20
  ወይሁቦ), which carries a mid-right dash.
- ጸ = narrow-waisted body with a top stem; ዘ = H with a top tick. Confirmed
  distinct at 4x (l.3 vs l.8).
- The ። at ll.4, 8, 10, 12 and 24 is the two-pair form with four red dots
  around it; at l.7 it is the plain two-pair form. Both kept as ።.

No Arabic loanwords suspected on this page; the unplaceable words (ወባቁሙ,
እልፉ, ያዐርሞ) are Ge'ez-shaped, not loan-shaped.
