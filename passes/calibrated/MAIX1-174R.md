## WORK

A church-order canon on rank, addressed in the second person singular to a bishop/teacher, not a homily. The ink gives:

- l.4-7: ወአንተ፡ሠራዕ፡ሎሙ ... ሕግ፡ቀኖና፡ዘቀደስተ፡ቤተ፡ክርስቲያን፡ዘሠራዕከ፡ሎሙ — "and you, ordain for them ... the law of the canon of the holy church which you ordained for them." The addressee is the same single teacher/shepherd that ff.168-173 address, so this is the same work continuing, now in its ordinance section.
- l.8-19: a descending hierarchy in which no rank may exalt itself (ኢይትዐበይ) over the one above it: lesser/greater (ንዑስ/ዓቢይ), priest/bishop (ካህን/ጳጳስ), deacon/priest, subdeacon and reader (ንፍቀ፡ዲያቆናት, አናጉንስጢስ)/deacons, layman (ሕዝባዊ)/reader, women/men, wives/husbands (ዓመተቲሆን = አምታቲሆን), children/virgins, then a further female class (ዕቢራት, unresolved, widows expected by sense).
- l.20-24: sanction and manner: that God not be angry with them (fem. pl. object, ኢይትመዓዖን), "with much devotion and prayer, with fear of God, when they assemble."

Against the Didascalia ch.2 (= AC II.5-6) hypothesis: this page does not fit it. The Didascalia's ranked household is bishop, presbyter, deacon, deaconess, widow, orphan (ch.9 = AC II.26); it has no subdeacon and the reader is marginal. ንፍቀ፡ዲያቆን and አናጉንስጢስ as fixed rungs between deacon and layman belong to the 4th-century and later church orders: Apostolic Constitutions VIII (Canons of Simon, can. 28), Canons of Hippolytus, Testamentum Domini, and the Ethiopic Sinodos compilations built on them. Rank-humility clauses in this "let not X exalt over Y" form, running down through women, wives, children and widows, are Sinodos material (the ordinance canons attributed to the apostles/Clement). So: an Ethiopic apostolic church-order compilation of Sinodos type, later than the Didascalia proper; I cannot name the exact canon or match it verbatim. No scriptural quotation on this page; the wives/husbands clause echoes Eph 5:22 but is not a citation, so nothing to record on verse order.

Arabic loanwords: none found. ንፍቀ (ንፍቅ, half) and አናጉንስጢስ (Greek anagnostes) are native/Greek, not Arabic.

## GIST

The bishop is told to enforce the canon of the holy church he laid down, so that no rank exalts itself over the one above it: priest over bishop, deacon over priest, subdeacon and reader over deacon, layman over reader, women over men, wives over their husbands, children over virgins, and (widows?) likewise. This is so that God be not angry with them, and that they serve with much devotion and prayer in fear of God when they assemble.
