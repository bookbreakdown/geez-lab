## WORK

**Apostolic church-order paraenesis in the first-person-plural command voice, on
conduct at the liturgy. Not Apocalypse of Paul. Same frame as ff.168–175r.**

Evidence from the ink, diagnostic first:

1. **l.4–5 ወከዕበ፡እዘዝነ = ወካዕበ፡አዘዝነ, "and again we command."** A vision text
   (Apocalypse of Paul, Ra'ǝyä Maryam) never speaks in the apostolic "we
   command"; the church-order corpus (Apostolic Constitutions / the Sinodos
   ትእዛዛት, the Testament of Our Lord, the Clementine ordinances) does, and uses
   ወካዕበ as the rule-to-rule connector. This is the same voice as ff.168–174.
2. **l.1–2 እምስብሐተ፡ነግህ, "from the morning praise" (matins)** — a canonical-hours
   term; the page regulates behaviour inside a service, not a tour of paradise.
3. **The rule content is a known church-order rule**: nobody leaves the church
   before the priest concludes (ዘእንበለ፡ይፍትም፡ካህን, three times: ll.7, 13, 16),
   with the simile of the man who stands before the king, makes a request and
   walks out before receiving it (ll.8–11); and no talking with one's neighbour
   in church (ll.19–23). The "dismissal" rule and the king simile belong to the
   Sinodos / Fetha Nagast lane of Ethiopic canon literature, not to any
   apocalypse.
4. **What is absent**: no vision framing (no "and I saw", no angel, no river,
   no "the apostle"), no proper name, no citation formula. If 175v had truly
   entered Apocalypse of Paul, 176v would still be in Paul's tour (ch. 23 leads
   into the City of Christ and the punishments, chs. 24–44). It is not.

So: **the page does not fit an Apocalypse-of-Paul frame.** Either 175v's river
list was a paradise digression inside the church-order text (the four rivers
in Apoc. Paul order circulate independently in Ethiopic homiletic material), or
the codex has switched back. The catalogue label "Qalementos" deserves a
second look rather than dismissal: the Ethiopic Mäṣḥafä Qälemǝnṭos is exactly
the kind of compilation that carries apostolic ordinances in the "we command"
voice alongside eschatological set pieces, and a codex that "changes works
without announcing it" is what a Qalementos looks like from inside. I cannot
confirm that from this page alone; what this page does confirm is the genre.

## GIST

The page opens mid-rule: something about a priest/holy one at the morning
office withdrawing himself so that there is no tumult, each keeping what is his
(ll.1–4, section closed with a red-dotted ።). "And again we command": having
entered from the [?], let no one leave the church before the priest concludes,
for he is like a man who stands before the king, asks him for [ተከዘ] and walks
out before receiving it; likewise whoever leaves the church before the priest
concludes — what did you come for, and what did you take away, you who left
before the priest finished? Let your entering not be slow and your leaving
quick; and when you enter the church, keep hold of yourself [ተከዘ] until you
[would] talk with your neighbour — and whoever talks in the church has forfeited
[ተከዘ] without forgiveness of sins.
