## WORK

Not the Apocalypse of Paul frame that f.175v set up, on the diagnostic evidence below. The page is church-order / canonical paraenesis, and it closes on the word ቀኖና "canon" (l.22).

Evidence from the ink, strongest first:

1. **A creedal digest in narrative order (ll.10-15):** his death, his resurrection, his ascension into heaven, and again his coming to judge the living and the dead and repay everyone according to his works. This is the ordered kerygma that opens church-order prefaces (the Sinodos / Apostolic Constitutions type already identified at ff.168-174). The Apocalypse of Paul has no creedal digest anywhere.
2. **ከመዝ፡ቀኖና (l.22)** and the repeated ቤተ፡ክርስቲያን (ll.17-18, 20-21, 21-22): the vocabulary of canons, not of a heavenly tour.
3. **Second-person singular address** (ኢትስማዕ, ኢትናገር, ትከዘ, ርእስከ, ትስርቅ, ትእዘዝ): the prohibitive canon frame ("you shall not..."), the same voice as the widows' and almsgiving sections at ff.174v-175r. Apoc. Paul's second person is an angel addressing Paul about what he sees.
4. **The prophets-then-apostles chain of transmission (ll.5-9):** the prophets "who told what was and what will be", then "he told the apostles, who told us how..." is the church-order authority formula (prophets → Christ → apostles → us), not apocalyptic narrative.
5. **A scriptural allusion, not a heavenly river:** "its taste like honeycomb" (ll.4-5, ጸቃውዐ፡መዓር, Ps 19:10 / 119:103 language). The honey here is a simile for the word of God, not the river of honey of Apoc. Paul 23. So the honey overlap with f.175v is lexical only.

What this means for the frame: either f.175v's four-rivers passage was itself a quotation inside the church-order text (the Sinodos-type compilations do embed such material), or the codex changed works again between 175v and 177r. This page cannot decide which; f.176r-v must. The catalogue label (Qalementos IV) is not excluded by this page: the Ethiopic Qalementos books IV-VII are precisely canon material in second-person address, and ርእስከ "yourself" is compatible with Peter-to-Clement address. But nothing on this page names Peter or Clement, so that remains the catalogue's claim, not the ink's.

Citation order: the creed sequence runs forward (death → resurrection → ascension → parousia → judgment → recompense). No book of scripture is named on the page; the honeycomb allusion and the "prophets told what was and what will be" line are allusive, not cited.

Arabic loans: none claimed. እስራበ (l.6) is unplaced but no root is offered, so no loan is asserted.

## GIST

The page opens on the man who finds fault with God and despises his word, and sets against him the word of God and of the prophets, sweet as honeycomb and flowing like pure gold, transmitted by the prophets who told what was and what will be, and by the apostles who told us of Christ's death, resurrection, ascension, return to judge the living and the dead, and repayment of each by his works. It then turns to second-person prohibition: do not listen to nor speak (with those?) in the church so that you grieve(?) yourself; and further, of Satan's ... you rob (or: shine on?) the church and you ... the church; "thus is the canon..." (breaking off at ከ).

Held loosely: ll.19-22 are the least certain stretch, and ትስርቅ could be ሠረቀ "rise/shine" with the feminine ቤተ፡ክርስቲያን as subject rather than ሰረቀ "steal"; the transcription stays with the ink (ስ).

## CONFIDENCE

- **Top third (high):** ll.7-15. ንጹሕ, the prophets/apostles chain, the creedal digest, ወይፈድዮ፡ለኵሉ፡በከመ፡ምግባሩ. Each word is a standard Ge'ez word in a formula whose next word was predictable before it was read.
- **Middle third:** ll.2-6 and ll.16-18. Content words certain (ቃለ፡እግዚአብሔር, ጸቃውዐ፡መዓር, ነቢያት, ቤተ፡ክርስቲያን, ኢትናገር), but ወይሙንን, ድርስን, እስራበ and ትከዘ are letter-by-letter reads with unresolved sense.
- **Bottom third (low):** l.1 w.1 (ዘያስተንቁፎ), ll.19-22. One glyph on l.19 is unread (?), ስይጠን rests on a sense argument across the line break, and ትስርቅ / ወትእዘዝ have two live parses.
