VERDICT UP FRONT
----------------
The sign is  ፳  =  TWENTY.
The page reads: Othniel son of Kenaz, brother of Caleb — **20 years**.

It is NOT ፵ (40) and NOT ፴ (30). Both are excluded by a positive visible
feature, not by silhouette. 50 cannot be excluded by the anchor table (which has
no ፶ shape) but IS excluded by the sign's own attested compounds on this folio.

Confidence in the SIGN IDENTITY (this glyph = the glyph at 42r ll.7 and 22):
  high — reading, not inferring.
Confidence in the VALUE (that sign = 20):
  high, but inherited from SCRIBAL-KEY.md's triple anchor, not re-derived here.

1. LOCATING THE FIGURE — THE SURROUNDING TEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Located by reading, not by line number. Counting from the top of the written
block, the figure falls on the 15th line, but the locating evidence is the text.

  line 14:  ወእምድኅሬሁ ፡ ጐቶንያል ፡ ወልደ ፡
            "and after him, Gothonyal (Othniel), son of"

  line 15:  ቄኔዝ ፡ እኂሁ ፡ ለካሌብ ፡ 【FIGURE】 ቃመት ፡
            "Kenaz, his brother — of Caleb — [N] years"

  line 16:  ት ፡ ወእምድኅሬሁ ፡ ተቀነዩ ፡ …
            "…and after him they were subjected…"

Transcription notes (ink, not normalised, per the key):
  - ጐቶንያል  = LXX Γοθονιηλ, Othniel. Line 14, immediately before the break.
  - እኂሁ    for standard እኁሁ. This hand marks ኀ third order with the ኢ-style
             left-curl (SCRIBAL-KEY.md, "Letterforms that collide"). There is a
             brown ink blot over the second glyph of this word; the word is
             still legible from its frame and from the ሁ that follows.
  - ቃመት    = ዓመት "years". This hand's ዓ is drawn as a 9-shaped bowl that reads
             as ቃ at any magnification; ቃመት is how "years" is written in every
             chronology entry on this folio (ll. 7, 8, 9, 11, 13, 15, 18, 22).
             Transcribed as inked.
  - The figure stands ALONE between the word divider and ቃመት. Verified on a
     760 px wide context crop (42R-othniel-context-4x.png): there is no second
     barred sign before or after it, and no ወ-conjunction compound. It is a bare
     tens figure with no unit — unlike l.7 (፻ወ፳) and l.22 (፳ወ፯).

Sequence check, which confirms the slot is Othniel's:
     l.13  ኵንኖሙ ፡ ፰ ቃመት        Cushan-Rishathaim, 8 years
     l.15  [TARGET]              Othniel  ← the target
     l.18  ፲፰ ቃመት                Eglon, 18 years
Cushan before, Eglon after. This is Othniel's figure.

2. MORPHOLOGY OF THE SIGN (at 10x — 42R-othniel-fig-8x.png, stack-tens.png)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bounding bars — checked first, per the most load-bearing rule in the key:
  - ABOVE: a thin ruled bar with a filled orange terminal at each end.
  - BELOW: a thin ruled bar with a filled orange terminal at each end.
  Both present. This is a numeral, not a letter. The orange terminals here are
  flat blobs on the ends of a bar, NOT the eight-point orange/black rosettes
  that punctuate this page (those appear at l.12 left margin, l.13 end, l.18
  mid-line, l.22 mid-line and are decoration/punctuation).

The glyph itself, top to bottom:
  - A heavy horizontal CAPPING BAR, drawn as part of the letter in black,
    distinctly wider than the body and OVERHANGING it on BOTH sides. The left
    overhang is a flat wing about a third of the body width; the right overhang
    is a slightly thicker wing that rises very faintly at its tip.
  - From under the middle of that bar the body hangs as TWO STROKES ONLY:
      * left stroke: thinner, leaves the bar near-vertically then bows outward
        to the left as it descends, ending in a small leftward flare at the foot;
      * right stroke: heavier, broader, descends with a mild outward splay to the
        right, ending in a blunt squared foot.
    The two strokes MEET at the top, under the bar, forming a narrow neck — the
    counter is closed above.
  - COUNTER: a single pear / teardrop aperture, narrow at the top neck, widest
    at the bottom, its long axis vertical, tilted very slightly left. Clean
    parchment inside, no ink bridge.
  - BASELINE: a thin horizontal hairline runs between the two feet and closes
    the counter at the bottom, projecting a short way to the LEFT of the left
    foot. The counter is therefore closed all round.
  - NOTHING descends below the baseline. No stem, no tail, no foot-hook,
    no third prong, no mid-stroke, no ring, no tick.

Summary silhouette: capping bar with wings, over a closed two-legged arch.
Exactly the key's "bar + bowl + leg" for ፳, seen closely.

3. VALUE, SECOND CHOICE, AND WHAT SEPARATES THEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VALUE:         ፳  = 20
SECOND CHOICE: ፶  = 50   (see §4 — no visible feature separates these two; the
                          separation is by the sign's own compounds)

THIRTY and FORTY are both excluded by a POSITIVE VISIBLE FEATURE, which is the
thing the documented low-tens failure is supposed to be guarded by:

  PRONG COUNT.  SCRIBAL-KEY.md describes BOTH ፴ and ፵ as "three-pronged" —
  they are one family, separated from each other only by the descending right
  stem. The target has TWO strokes, not three. It never enters that family, so
  the 30/40 discrimination does not even have to be run.

  Counted directly at 10x, and against the page's own ፵ at ll.8 and 9:
     ፵ at l.8  — left block, V-notch, middle peak, second notch, right stroke
                 that DESCENDS WELL BELOW THE BASELINE and ends in a foot.
                 Three prongs, one descender. (42R-othniel-stack-tens.png,
                 bottom-right panel.)
     TARGET    — bar, two strokes, one closed counter, feet joined, no descender.
  These are not variants of one sign. They share no stroke sequence.

POSITIVE identification, not merely negative — this is what settles it:
  The target is the SAME SIGN, stroke for stroke, as two other figures on this
  same folio, same pen, same sitting, whose values are fixed by the companion
  signs they stand with:

     42r l.7   ፻ ወ 【SAME SIGN】 ቃመت     = 120   (bowl=100, conj. ወ, sign)
     42r l.22  በ 【SAME SIGN】 ወ ፯ ቃመት   = 27    (sign, conj. ወ, ገ-with-foot=7)
     42r l.15  【TARGET】 ቃመት             = the figure under examination

  Stacked at identical 10x magnification in
  crops/42R-othniel-stack-tens.png (top-left = target, top-right = l.7,
  bottom-left = l.22, bottom-right = the l.8 ፵ for contrast).
  All three arch-forms show: overhanging capping bar; two-stroke body; closed
  pear counter; joined feet; no descender. The only differences are pen
  variation — the l.22 example has a longer bar and a rounder, more oval
  counter; the l.7 example has a slightly heavier foot bar. The l.7 and l.22
  signs stand inside compounds that make them tens digits, and the value that
  makes both compounds work is 20.

  In 120 the sign is the tens digit after ፻. In 27 it is the tens digit before
  ፯. 120 and 27 are the values SCRIBAL-KEY.md anchors for those two lines
  (against d'Abbadie 78 via Grébaut). I did not re-derive them; I verified that
  the SIGN in them is the same sign as my target.

THE STRONGEST INTERNAL ARGUMENT, and it is witness-free:
  **This scribe writes ፵ elsewhere on this very page — three times.**
  ll.8 and 9 each carry a barred ፵ ("40 years"), and l.23 carries ፵፻ in
  "ተፈጸመ … ፵፻ እም ፍጥረተ [ዓለም]" — 4000 years completed from the creation of the
  world. So the ፵ form was in the pen, in this sitting, within seven lines
  above and eight lines below the target, and it looks nothing like the target.
  The l.15 figure is not a badly-made ፵. It is a different sign the same hand
  makes a different way, repeatedly, on the same leaf.

WHAT THIS MEANS, stated plainly because it is the point of the exercise:
  Judges 3:11 gives Othniel FORTY years. **This manuscript does not.** It gives
  the 20-sign. That is a real divergence in the ink, not a reading artefact, and
  it survives every check I can run on the page. Qalementos is not the Masoretic
  text and is entitled to disagree; what matters here is that the disagreement
  is IN THE INK and is not the documented low-tens bias, because the low-tens
  bias operates inside the three-pronged family and this sign is outside it.

4. CAN 50 BE EXCLUDED?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOT BY THE TABLE. SCRIBAL-KEY.md §"GAP IN THE ANCHOR TABLE" is correct and the
gap is real: there is no recorded shape for ፶ in this hand, so no shape test can
reject 50. I want to be explicit that I did not invent one. I also note a second
gap the key half-conceals: **፴ has no anchor either** — its anchor cell in the
table is EMPTY, and its "three-pronged, stemless" description is an inference by
analogy from ፵, not an observation. Of the tens, only ፲, ፳ and ፵ are anchored.

EXCLUDED BY THE SIGN'S OWN COMPOUNDS, which is a different and better test:
  The target sign is the same sign that stands as the tens digit in 42r l.7 and
  42r l.22. If it were 50, those two lines would read 150 and 57. Per the key
  the same sign on 42v l.8 gives Tola son of Puah 23 years, which is Judges 10:2
  verbatim — a scriptural anchor, and the key records that our own English
  edition independently reads twenty-three there. On a reading of 50, Tola
  becomes 53 and contradicts scripture on a folio that otherwise tracks Judges
  entry by entry. Three compounds, one consistent value, and one of them
  scripturally anchored. 50 is excluded.

  Honest accounting: that exclusion is inherited, not re-derived. My own
  page-local, witness-free contribution is (a) the sign identity across ll.7,
  15 and 22, and (b) the exclusion of the entire three-pronged family. The step
  from "this arch sign" to "the value 20" rests on the key's anchors.

FOR THE RECORD — no third tens shape exists on this folio. Every barred figure
on 42r was examined. The inventory is:
     l.7   ፻ ወ ፳            = 120
     l.8   ፵                = 40
     l.9   ፵                = 40
     l.11  two figures (three-pronged + an I-beam-with-bowl form; not re-read
           here, outside the brief)
     l.13  ፰                = 8    (Cushan)
     l.15  ፳                = 20   ← TARGET
     l.18  ፲ ፰              = 18   (Eglon; this is the pair earlier misread as
                                     108 by taking the ፲ I-beam for a ፻ bowl)
     l.21  one figure (not re-read)
     l.22  ፳ ወ ፯            = 27
     l.23  ፵ ፻              = 4000 (from the creation of the world)
Only three tens forms occur: the I-beam (፲), the arch (፳), the three-pronged
descender (፵). There is no fourth, unassigned tens shape on this page that
could be a ፶ waiting to be anchored.

5. THE ON-PAGE CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two controls were built, both from THIS page, THIS pen, THIS sitting, and both
stacked with the target at identical magnification.

CONTROL A — the letter ብ of ለካሌብ, the word immediately before the figure.
  crops/42R-othniel-control-bet-vs-fig.png   (left = ብ, right = target, 10x)
  Chosen because it is the nearest silhouette on the page — bar over two legs
  over a counter — and because it sits four glyphs from the target, so pen
  pressure, ink load and rule spacing are identical.
  WHAT IT SHOWED, four separators, all visible:
    (i)   the ብ has NO bounding bars; the target has both. Decisive on its own.
    (ii)  the ብ's top bar is FLUSH with its two verticals — it does not overhang.
          The target's capping bar overhangs on both sides.
    (iii) the ብ's two strokes are PARALLEL and vertical; the target's SPLAY
          outward from a neck.
    (iv)  the ብ's counter is rectangular and OPEN at the bottom — the two legs
          stand separately on the line. The target's counter is a closed pear,
          shut by a hairline joining the feet.
  The control's real value is the reverse direction: it proves I can resolve
  splay, overhang and foot-closure at this magnification, which are the same
  three features the ፳-identification rests on.

CONTROL B — the letter ሠ of ንጉሠ ("king"), line 17, two lines below the target.
  crops/42R-othniel-control-sawt-vs-40-vs-fig.png
  (left = ሠ letter, centre = ፵ numeral from l.8, right = target, all 10x)
  This is the control the three-pronged trap actually calls for.
  WHAT IT SHOWED:
    - ሠ: three prongs, all three feet ON the baseline, flat-bottomed,
      no descender, NO bounding bars.
    - ፵: the same three-pronged skeleton, but the right prong DESCENDS WELL
      BELOW the baseline and ends in a foot, and the whole sign is bar-bounded
      above and below.
      So the ሠ letter is the exact silhouette the key warns ፴ would present,
      and putting it beside the ፵ shows precisely what the descending stem adds.
      This is the 30/40 discriminator, demonstrated on the page rather than
      quoted from the table.
    - TARGET: two strokes. It does not belong to either. The discriminator was
      never needed, and reaching for it — matching "three-pronged" and then
      arguing about the stem — is how this figure would have been read as a
      tens value in the 30/40 band that it plainly is not.

6. CONFIDENCE — AND WHERE I AM READING vs INFERRING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
READING (from the ink, high confidence):
  - The figure carries bounding bars above and below. It is a numeral.
  - It stands alone, with no adjoining sign and no ወ-compound.
  - Its body has TWO strokes, a closed pear counter, joined feet, an overhanging
    capping bar, and nothing below the baseline.
  - It is the same sign as the tens digit at 42r l.7 and at 42r l.22.
  - It is NOT the sign written at 42r ll.8, 9 and 23, which this same scribe
    wrote three times on this same leaf.
  - The surrounding text is the Othniel entry, bracketed by Cushan (l.13, 8) and
    Eglon (l.18, 18).

INFERRING (declared as such):
  - That the arch sign carries the value 20. This rests on SCRIBAL-KEY.md's
    anchors for l.7 (=120), l.22 (=27) and 42v l.8 (Tola 23 / Judges 10:2), not
    on anything I verified from an independent witness.
  - That ፴ would look like the ሠ silhouette with bars. The key says so; the
    table's ፴ cell is empty, so this is the key's inference, and I am passing it
    on labelled. It does not affect the verdict, since the target is outside
    that family either way.

I am NOT refusing a value. The ink supports one and supports it well: the sign is
formed cleanly, is undamaged, is repeated twice more on the folio in contexts
that fix its value, and is contrasted three times on the folio with the sign it
would have to be mistaken for.

THE ONE THING THAT WOULD OVERTURN THIS: an anchored ፶ shape that turns out to be
a bar-over-closed-arch. That is the live risk and it is the same gap found
yesterday. Until a ፶ is anchored somewhere in this manuscript, this reading —
and every tens reading in this hand — carries that single residual exposure, and
the honest form of the claim is: **the Othniel figure is the 20-sign; the 20-sign
is 20 on three compounds including one scriptural anchor; and no ፶ shape is on
record to contest it.**

RECOMMENDED ADDITION TO SCRIBAL-KEY.md (not applied — not my call):
  ፳ deserves a fuller shape description than "bar + bowl + leg", which under-
  describes it and is what let an arch be confused with a three-pronged form.
  Proposed: "overhanging capping bar over a TWO-stroke splayed arch; counter a
  closed pear, widest at the bottom; feet joined by a hairline; nothing below
  the baseline. Two strokes, never three." And ፴'s empty anchor cell should be
  marked UNANCHORED in the same way ፶ now is.
