0. HEADLINE
====================================================================

SITE B (f.42v, Abimelech) CARRIES NO ፴.  It is not a pronged sign at all:
one vertical stem with a single upper-right arm.  Settled, high confidence.

SITE A (f.42r, the son of Nun) CARRIES A TENS SIGN THAT IS NOT ፲, NOT ፳,
NOT ፵ AND NOT ፻.  It has TWO prongs, not three.  It is therefore ፴ or ፶ —
the two empty cells — and the ink cannot separate them, because separating
them needs exactly the anchor that is missing.  What this run DOES deliver
is the first recorded morphology for whichever of the two it is, described
below in anchor-ready form, plus the hard negative that the sign in the
30/50 slot on this leaf is NOT three-pronged.

That negative is itself a correction to the key: the key asserts "፴ and ፵
are a three-pronged family".  On this folio the sign that must be ፴ or ፶
is two-pronged.  Either ፴ is not in the three-pronged family, or this is
፶ and ፶ is a two-pronged stem-bearing form.  Do not keep asserting the
family claim for ፴ as if it were observed.  It never has been.

2. MORPHOLOGY — PRONG COUNT FIRST, THEN STEMS
====================================================================

--- SITE A, N1 (the first term, f.42r l.11) ------------------------
crops: thirty-A-N1-9x.png, thirty-cmp-montage.png, thirty-A-N1-baseline.png

  PRONGS: TWO.  Two limbs, ONE valley between them.  The valley is a
  small U/V notch that OPENS UPWARD at the top of the sign, roughly a
  third of the way across from the left; it is a genuine gap in the ink,
  clean-edged, not a fill defect.  There is no second valley anywhere on
  the top edge.  Total width is visibly narrower than every ፵ on either
  leaf, consistent with one fewer prong rather than with a merged one.

  CAPS: each limb carries its own short flat cap bar, and the two caps
  sit at DIFFERENT heights (left cap higher).  The left cap overhangs to
  the LEFT only, ending in a downward wedge tip; it does not overhang to
  the right.  There is NO single wide cap spanning both limbs, and no
  right-hand wing.

  COUNTER: NONE.  The notch is open to the air above.  Nothing is
  enclosed anywhere in the sign.

  FEET / BASELINE: left limb ends in a flat foot that stops SHORT of the
  line's baseline.  The right limb runs down past it and lands ON the
  baseline, level with the feet of the adjacent ወ and of N2.  So the
  right limb is longer than the left — a stem in the relative sense —
  but it does NOT descend below the baseline.  There is NO hairline bar
  joining the two feet.

  INK: solid, evenly loaded, no blotting; the lower-left quadrant is
  filled black, which is why at 2x the left limb reads as a single broad
  mass.  Bounding bars present ABOVE and BELOW (thin brown rules with
  orange rosettes at the ends) — it is unambiguously a numeral.

--- SITE A, N2 (the second term, f.42r l.11) -----------------------
crops: thirty-A-N1-9x.png (right panel), thirty-A-N1-baseline.png

  PRONGS: none — it is a closed ring.
  Upright, roughly rectangular body; two thick near-parallel sides; a
  tall closed OVAL counter running nearly the full body height; a small
  ear/overhang at the TOP LEFT only; the bottom closes across.  A short
  tail at bottom left.  Height equal to the neighbouring ወ.  Nothing
  projects below the closure.  Bounding bars above and below.

--- SITE B, B-FIG (f.42v l.7) --------------------------------------
crops: thirty-B-montage.png, thirty-bowl-compare.png

  PRONGS: ZERO.  This is not a pronged sign and not a member of the
  30/40/50 family in any form.
  ONE vertical stem on the left, full height, ending in a broad flat
  foot.  From the top of the stem a single ARM runs right and tapers to
  a fine point.  From under the arm a wedge returns down-and-left and
  meets the stem at about mid-height.  The area between the arm and the
  returning wedge is OPEN to the right — the arm tip and the wedge do
  not meet, so nothing is enclosed.  Below the junction the stem
  continues to the foot.  Bounding bars above and below.

3. VALUES, SECOND CHOICES, AND THE SEPARATING FEATURE
====================================================================

SITE A, N1
  Reading:        a tens sign, ፴ or ፶ — NOT resolvable on this page.
  First choice:   ፶ = 50.
  Second choice:  ፴ = 30.
  Separating feature I would use, if an anchor existed: prong count.
  Why I lean ፶: every description of ፴ available anywhere in this key
  puts it in the three-pronged family, and this sign has two prongs
  with a lengthened right limb.  If ፴ really is three-pronged, this is
  not ፴, and ፶ is the only tens cell left.
  Why that lean is WEAK and must be labelled as such: the key's ፴ entry
  is explicitly a description-by-contrast with ፵, never an observation.
  Excluding ፴ by it is excluding a sign by a guess.  If the true ፴ of
  this hand is two-pronged, this is ፴ and the family claim is wrong.
  NOTHING on these two leaves can break that.  Confidence in the value:
  LOW (~55/45).  Confidence in the morphology: HIGH.
  POSITIVELY EXCLUDED, and these are the grounds:
    ፲  — N1 has two limbs, a notch and two caps.  ፲ is a plain serifed
         I-beam with no notch.  Excluded on silhouette.
    ፳  — ፳ has ONE wide cap bar winged on BOTH sides, a closed
         pear/triangular counter, feet joined by a hairline, and nothing
         below the baseline.  N1 has two separate caps at different
         heights, NO counter at all, NO hairline foot-bar, and a right
         limb longer than the left.  Excluded on four independent
         features.  This matters: the two previous wrong 30-readings on
         these leaves were ፳.  This sign is not that sign.
    ፵  — three prongs, two valleys, and a right stem that plunges
         clearly BELOW the feet of its neighbours.  N1 has two prongs,
         one valley, and a right limb that only reaches the baseline.
         Excluded on prong count, which per the key is the primary test.
    ፻  — ፻ is a stem with an arm and a CLOSED counter (see §5).  N1 has
         no stem-and-arm construction and no counter.  Excluded.

SITE A, N2
  First choice:   ፪ = 2.  The key's ፪ is "loop, not a Z"; N2 is a closed
                  loop with a top-left ear and nothing else.
  Second choice:  ፻ = 100.
  Separating feature: ፻ is a stem that CONTINUES BELOW its bowl to a
  separate foot, with the bowl occupying only the upper right (see the
  42r l.7 control, §5).  N2's counter fills the whole body and no stem
  projects below it.  Also the slot argues: the scribe writes compounds
  larger-first, so the term after ወ is the smaller one; a hundred cannot
  follow a tens here.
  So Site A reads [30 or 50] ወ [2] ዓመት — i.e. thirty-two or fifty-two.
  Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH on N2 = 2; LOW on the tens, as above.

SITE B, B-FIG
  First choice:   ፫ = 3.
  Second choice:  ፻ = 100.
  Separating feature — and this is the key's own criterion, applied and
  it works: RHO'S BOWL MUST CLOSE BACK ONTO THE STEM.  Put the Abimelech
  sign beside the first term of the 120 on f.42r l.7 (crops/
  thirty-bowl-compare.png) and the difference is not subtle:
    42r l.7 (=the hundreds of 120): the returning stroke rejoins the
      stem and a small white triangle is FULLY ENCLOSED.  Closed bowl.
    42v l.7 (Abimelech): the arm tapers to a free point and the white
      runs out to the right.  OPEN hook.  Nothing enclosed.
  Two signs of the same family, same pen, one page apart, one closed and
  one open.  ፻ closed, ፫ open.
  Confidence: HIGH that these are two different signs and that the
  Abimelech one is the open member.  MEDIUM-HIGH that open = ፫ = 3.

4. ANCHOR-READY DESCRIPTION  (for the key, whichever cell it fills)
====================================================================

Put this in the ፶ row, with the caveat that it may belong in the ፴ row,
and say so in the row.  Either way the SHAPE is now on record, which it
was not before, and a future reader can match a sign against it blind:

  TWO-PRONGED, OPEN-NOTCHED, LONGER RIGHT LIMB.
  Two limbs side by side, each with its own short flat cap bar, the LEFT
  cap set HIGHER than the right and overhanging to the LEFT only, ending
  in a downward wedge tip.  Between the limbs a single clean U-notch
  that OPENS UPWARD; no second notch, no enclosed counter anywhere.  The
  left limb stops SHORT of the baseline; the right limb runs past it and
  lands ON the baseline — longer than its neighbour but NOT descending
  below the line.  No hairline bar joins the feet.  Overall width
  noticeably narrower than ፵ in the same hand.
  Attestation: f.42r, the son-of-Nun entry, first term of [N]ወ፪ዓመት.

  TESTS THAT SEPARATE IT FROM ITS NEIGHBOURS, in the order to apply them:
   1. Count prongs.  Three prongs -> ፴/፵ family, not this.  Two -> this
      or ፳.
   2. Is anything enclosed?  A closed pear/triangular counter -> ፳.
      Open notch -> this.
   3. Is there ONE wide cap winged both sides, or TWO separate caps at
      different heights?  One wide winged cap -> ፳.  Two caps -> this.
   4. Do the feet sit on a hairline bar?  Yes -> ፳.  No -> this.
   5. Does the right limb go BELOW the baseline?  Yes -> ፵.  Level with
      it -> this.

6. CAN ፶ BE EXCLUDED?
====================================================================

  SITE B: YES, decisively, and without needing a ፶ anchor.  Whatever ፶
  looks like, it is a tens sign, and every tens sign in this system is
  built from prongs on a baseline.  The Abimelech figure has no prongs
  at all — it is a single vertical stem carrying one upper-right arm,
  the same construction as ፻ and ፫.  You do not need to know a sign's
  shape to exclude it from a different construction class.  ፴ is
  excluded at Site B on exactly the same ground.

  SITE A: NO, and this is the whole finding.  ፶ cannot be excluded at
  Site A because the table still contains no shape for it; and ፴ cannot
  be excluded either, except by a ፴ description that was never observed.
  The gap is now HALF closed: the sign in that slot has a recorded
  morphology, and four of the five tens are positively separated from it
  by named features.  What remains is a one-bit ambiguity — 30 against
  50 — and closing it needs a figure on another folio whose value is
  fixed by scripture or by our own English, not more pixels from f.42r.

  RECOMMENDED NEXT MOVE: hunt the two-pronged open-notch form on a leaf
  where the value is externally pinned.  It is a distinctive silhouette
  and now describable, so it can be searched for.  One sighting in an
  anchored slot closes both empty cells at once, because whichever it
  is, the other is then the three-pronged stemless form by elimination.
