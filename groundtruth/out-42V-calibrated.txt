METHOD
Every doubtful glyph was measured against forms of the SAME scribe whose value is
fixed either by the calibration note or by an undisputed reading:
  ፵  = three-pronged body (ሠ-like) WITH a descending right stem (ሣ-like). 42v L1, L5, L17, L18, L20.
  ፴  = the same three-pronged body WITHOUT the stem. 42r L11 (Joshua, calibrated 31).
  ፳  = flat top bar, then a rounded bowl on the left and a straight leg on the right,
       both feet on the baseline. 42r L7 (Moses, calibrated 120), 42r L22 (calibrated 27),
       42v L8 (Tola). Three instances, identical.
  ፶  = flat top bar over a body whose left lobe and right leg CROSS. 42r L15 (Othniel,
       calibrated 50).
  ፻  = stem with a bowl at top right (P-like). 42r L7.
  ፯  = ገ-shape with a foot. 42v L3, L13, L15; 42r L22.
  ፰  = I-beam with a hook at mid-height on the right. 42r L13 (Cushan, 8).
  ፫  = stem with a closed bowl at top RIGHT, flared foot. 42v L8 (Tola units, 23).
  ፩  = rounded box with a top bar. 42r L11 (Joshua units, calibrated 1).
  ፲  = plain I-beam (serif top and bottom, nothing else). No calibrated instance exists on
       either leaf; identified by elimination — it is the only tens form left once ፳, ፴, ፵,
       ፶ and ፻ have their own distinct shapes above.

WHERE THIS READ DISAGREES WITH THE CALIBRATION NOTE
1. Abimelech (L7). The note wants ፴ (30). The scribe's ፴ is the stemless three-prong
   form (42r L11); the Abimelech glyph is a forked stem on a foot. The ink is a units
   glyph (፬ or ፫), not a tens glyph, unless this hand has a ፴ form unattested on
   either leaf.
2. Jair (L11). The note wants ፳ (22). The scribe's ፳ is the bar+bowl+leg form, used
   three times including one line above Jair's own figure (Tola). Jair's glyph is the
   plain bar. I tested ፳ and ፶ and cannot make the ink match either.
Both disagreements survive the "assume you are biased low" correction because they rest
on comparing shapes within the same hand, not on my prior for what ፲ or ፫ "should" look
like. If the calibration values came from a printed edition rather than from this
manuscript's ink, that is the likeliest source of the gap.
