# Ma IX 1 — scribal key for this hand

Accumulated from reads of ff. 41v, 42r, 42v, 153r, 154r, 155r, 156r, 156v,
157r, 157v on 2026-09-17. **Feed this into every transcription prompt.** Each
agent that rediscovers a letterform spends calls doing it; this file is how that
work compounds instead of repeating.

Ankobar, 1813-1847. Single column, 22-25 lines per side.

## Numerals — the highest-value section

### THE GENERATIVE RULE (found 2026-09-17, replaces the lookup table below)

**Ethiopic numerals are Greek uncial letters with bounding bars.** This is the
historical derivation (Greek via Coptic) and it turns this section from a list of
observed shapes into a system that predicts shapes never yet seen:

    Α1  Β2  Γ3  Δ4  Ε5  Ϛ6  Ζ7  Η8  Θ9
    Ι10 Κ20 Λ30 Μ40 Ν50 Ξ60 Ο70 Π80 Ϙ90  Ρ100

Every shape described below falls out of it:

| this key called it | it is | value |
|---|---|---|
| "mid-hooked I-beam" | **eta** Η | 8 |
| "plain serifed I-beam" | **iota** Ι | 10 |
| "bar + bowl + leg" | **kappa** Κ | 20 |
| "three-pronged, stemless" | **lambda** Λ | 30 |
| "three-pronged WITH descending right stem" | **mu** Μ | 40 |
| "ገ-shape with a foot" | **zeta** Ζ | 7 |
| "closed bowl" | **rho** Ρ | 100 |

**It also names the dangerous collisions in advance**, which the anchor table
could only discover one folio at a time:

- **Ι vs Η** — 10 against 8, a mid-stroke apart.
- **Ρ vs Ι** — 100 against 10. This one demonstrably bites: f.42r was read as
  **108** for Eglon where the witness says **18**.
- **Ι/Κ/Λ** — 10/20/30 are three adjacent Greek letters, so they are genuinely
  easy to confuse. **But do not read this as confirming a directional bias** —
  that finding is retracted below. The collision is real; the claim that readers
  systematically fall to the low side of it is not.

### THE RULE IS PARTIALLY FALSIFIED — the TENS do not follow it

Tested on f.42v, a page it was not derived from, against our own English.

**It failed on ፳.** The rule says 20 = kappa, a stem with two arms right. The
key's anchor table describes the observed ፳ as **"bar + bowl + leg"** — which is
not a kappa on any reading. The generative rule and the key's most-trusted
anchor describe the same sign incompatibly.

**Our edition settles it in the anchor's favour.** f.42v l.8 counts Tola son of
Puah, and our English reads "**twenty-three** years". So the sign is ፳ = 20 and
the reader's kappa-driven Λ = 30 was wrong. **The anchor is right; the Greek
shape for the tens is not.**

    USE THE RULE FOR THE UNITS (Α-Θ) AND ፻.  Verified: ፫ Γ, ፯ Ζ, ፰ Η, ፪ Β,
                                              ፭ Ε, ፻ Ρ.
    DO NOT USE IT FOR THE TENS.  ፳ demonstrably does not carry the kappa shape;
                                 ፲ and ፵ happen to resemble iota and mu, which
                                 is what made the rule look general.

Either the Ethiopic tens diverged from the Greek shapes, or this hand's kappa
has closed into an arch. Unresolved, and it does not matter for use: **read the
tens off the anchor table, the units off the rule.**

**Two collisions the rule DOES predict correctly, and one it misses:**

- **Ι/Η (10 vs 8)** — fired on f.42v l.11 and the rule WON, producing a units
  reading the anchor table has no shape for.
- **Ρ/Γ (100 vs 3)** — fired twice on f.42v. The anchor table cannot prevent it:
  it describes ፫ as "closed bowl top-right" and ፻ as "closed bowl", the same
  words for 3 and 100. The rule's positive criterion — *rho's bowl must close
  back onto the stem* — is what caught both.
- **Ϛ/Ζ (6 vs 7) — NOT predicted and live.** Three of fifteen figures on f.42v
  are Ζ-forms. Judges gives Jephthah 6 where the page reads 7. The only offered
  discriminator is "stigma curls, zeta's top stroke is straight", which is weak.
  **Open question for the expert.**

**Use the rule for units, the anchors for tens, and check both.**

### THE TENS ARE MOSTLY UNANCHORED — only ፲, ፳ and ፵ have real shapes

Found 2026-09-17. **፴ = 30 and ፶ = 50 both have EMPTY anchor cells.** The table
describes ፴ only as "three-pronged, STEMLESS" — which is a description by
contrast with ፵, not an independent shape — and has nothing at all for ፶.

**This explains a run of errors.** Two readings of 30 were produced on
f.42r/42v against an unanchored sign, and both were wrong: Othniel is ፳ (two
strokes) and Tola is ፳ (our English says twenty-three). A reader asked to choose
between ፴ and ፵ is choosing between one real shape and one absence.

**Treat every tens reading as narrowed, not settled, unless it is ፲, ፳ or ፵.**

### ፳ = 20 — EXPANDED, the old entry under-described it

Previously "bar + bowl + leg", which lets an arch be mistaken for a
three-pronged form. Fuller description from f.42r l.15:

**An overhanging capping bar with wings both sides; beneath it TWO strokes, not
three, splaying from a narrow neck; a closed pear-shaped counter widest at the
bottom; feet joined by a hairline; NOTHING below the baseline.**

**Prong count is a useful FIRST test but it does NOT partition the tens.**
Corrected within a day of being written: f.42r carries a tens sign in the 30/50
slot that is **two-pronged and is NOT ፳** — no enclosed counter, cap bars at
different heights, no joined foot-bar, where ፳ has a closed pear counter and a
hairline foot-bar. So "two prongs = ፳" is false.

**And the "፴ is three-pronged" claim was never observed.** It was derived by
contrast with ፵ and has been asserted ever since. Do not exclude ፴ with it.

Use prong count to separate ፵ (three prongs + descender) from the rest, then
distinguish the two-pronged signs on counter, cap height and foot-bar.

Anchored on this folio at l.7 (the tens of 120), l.15 (Othniel) and l.22 (the
tens of 27) — three instances of one sign, two of them confirmed by our own
English.

### GAP IN THE ANCHOR TABLE — ፶ = 50 has NO recorded shape

Found 2026-09-17 while reading f.41v l.23. A tens figure could be positively
narrowed to the 30/40 pair by the stem test, but **፶ could not be excluded,
because the table contains no shape for it.** This is the same structural
weakness that produced the ፳/፴ error: a discriminator can only separate the
signs it lists. Anyone reading further should treat every tens reading as
"30/40/50 narrowed to two" until a ፶ is anchored.

### ፯ = 7 CONFIRMED against scripture, closing the Ϛ/Ζ question for this hand

f.41v l.5 reads እስመ፡አውሰበ…ስልሞን፡ወልደ፡ዳዊት፡እንስተ፡**፯፻** — "Solomon son of David
married **700** wives", which is **1 Kings 11:3**. First figure in the
ground-truth set with a scriptural anchor. The Ζ-form is 7 here, so the Ϛ/Ζ (6
vs 7) collision opened on f.42v is resolved for this scribe.

**THE "STANDING LOW-TENS BIAS" IS RETRACTED. Do not correct upward for it.**

It was built on six disagreements with d'Abbadie 78, scored as if that were this
manuscript. It is not. Later readings with on-page controls give **Othniel = 20**
and **Abimelech = 3** from the ink — the two lowest of those six — so **two of
the six were a manuscript variant, not a misreading.** Only Moses' 120 and
Ehud's 27 were real reading errors.

**The retracted instruction did damage while it stood.** Told to test upward,
readers produced **30 for Othniel and 30 for Tola**, where the ink gives 20 in
both places. A correction for a bias that was not there pushed readings HIGH.

**Read the sign. Do not apply a directional prior in either direction.** Measure
against the ፵ and ፯ forms on the same page, which are anchored, and against the
letters in the same pen.

The ፳ identification is triple-anchored and is the most reliable thing here.

## Letterforms that collide in this hand

- **ይ and ደ are drawn nearly identically.** Disambiguate on sense, and say so.
- **ኢ** is አ with a left-curling right leg (anchor: ኢሳይያስ, 156r).
- **ብ** is a box; **ዝ** is an H with a curled top. They never collide.
- **ጌ** carries a ring; **ጊ** carries a left foot.
- **ጳ** is shaped like this hand's ኀ. Read it as ጳ only where a name demands it
  (ጳውሎስ).
- **አ/እ are not distinguishable**: the alef is the hooked form everywhere
  (እልቦ for አልቦ, ኃጢእቶሙ for ኃጢአቶሙ). Transcribe as እ and flag for the editor.
- **ኀ/ኅ/ኃ order is frequently unmarked.** Do not invent an order.
- **ት** carries a top-left tick; plain **ተ** does not.
- **ኀ third order** is marked with the same left-curl as ኢ (እኂሁ where standard
  is እኁሁ).
- The **ከ family** has a top-left serif, which is not a ring. Do not read it as
  one.
- **ዎ has two distinct shapes** on a single page.
- **ፉ is mistakable for the ፪ loop.** A numeral carries bounding bars above and
  below; a letter inside a word does not. Check for the bars before calling
  anything a numeral.
- **ነ vs ኑ — WIDENED, the earlier rule was wrong.** ነ has at least TWO shapes,
  and both occur on a single page (f.166v): the serifed I-beam (used for the
  suffix -ነ "our") AND the up-curling-foot form this key previously called ኑ,
  which on f.166v reads ነ every time (ወውሉድነ, ወነሥኡ). So the foot does NOT
  identify ኑ. Decide on sense and morphology, not on the foot, and say which
  you used. ን carries a foot too, and reads as ኀ at 2x.
- **The I-beam ነ is silhouette-identical to ፲.** Only the bounding bars separate
  them (f.165r l.11 ኮነት; f.166v, five times after እንስቲያ and ሀገር). See the
  numeral rule below — this is the single most dangerous collision in the hand.
- **ኵ** has its hook at the TOP RIGHT of the stem, not mid-stem.
- **ዑ** is a single bowl with hook; **ው** has two loops. Count the loops.
- **ጉ/ግ read as ት** in this hand (ጉ is drawn as ተ with a foot hook). This
  produced ርት/ውቱዝ until the anathema formula ርጉም፡ወውጉዝ forced the correction.
- **ቲ** is ተ with a right dash just below mid, near-colliding with ቱ. **ት**
  carries a foot.
- **ዐ/ዓ are undistinguished** (9-shaped bowl, right descender). **ጋ is
  7-shaped** and separable from ዓ by shape; a ዓ/ጌ collision is possible.
- **ረ and ሪ ARE distinct** - ሪ has a tick. But **red punctuation dots beside a
  ረ read falsely as ሬ/ሮ at 2x**; at 4x all three ring candidates on f.162v
  proved plain ረ. Zoom before adding a ring.
- **Line-initial ው in ውጉዝ** is drawn like a printed ቍ (single loop, tall stem,
  top-right hook), unlike the mid-line two-loop ው.
- **The vocative ኦ is written as the plain hooked alef** (ኦ ወልድየ appears as
  እወልድየ).
- **Ghost offset from the facing page.** Grey mirrored glyphs transfer from the
  opposite leaf and sit in the margins (f.171v ll.2-3, right margin). They are
  not text. Check colour and position before reading anything in a margin.
- **ድ is ደ with a small closed lower-right FOOT.** Promoted from candidate to
  rule: **seven attestations** across ff.184v (x4), 186v (ll.20, 21) and the
  186r foot, all sense-anchored. It reads as ዮ at 2x. This is the only reliable
  exit from the ደ/ይ/ድ collision.
- **Deletion is three horizontal strokes.** Self-corrections are interlinear
  superscript.
- **A numeral carries bounding bars above AND below — both, not one.** A letter
  inside a word does not, however loop-like. This is the guard against inventing
  a figure, and it is the most load-bearing rule in this file: five agents have
  come within one glyph of promoting a letter to a numeral.
  - **One red bar alone is NOT a figure marker.** f.169v l.16 has a stray single
    red bar over a ሌ with no numeral present.
  - Silhouette is never enough. ፵ and ሣ are identical in outline on f.169v; the
    I-beam ነ is identical to ፲ on ff.165r and 166v; ፉ resembles ፪ on f.160v.
    In every case only the bars separate them.
- **።** is often written as two spaced ፡ pairs, sometimes with red dots around
  it. Keep it as ።, never decompose to ፡፡.

## Orthography: this scribe is consistently nonstandard

Transcribe the ink, never normalise. Attested: ስልሞን, ኵኖሙ/ኮነኖሙ for ኰነኖሙ,
ስብእ for ሰብአ, ግብፅ with ፅ, ጌኖክ for ሄኖክ, ጸለኤ for ፀላኢ, ሠዕር for ሠዐር,
ኢየራሰሌም, ሔዋርያት, ንጊል for ወንጌል, እብያት for አብያት, ቀድመ for ቅድመ.

Sadis appears where standard orthography has other orders throughout. Further
attested: ሀልዩ for ሐልዩ, ቀድስት for ቅድስት, ነገሠት for ነገሥት, እኬንኖሙ for እኰንኖሙ,
እክሊለ, ደያቆናት, ቢተ for ቤተ after እም-, ሌቃናት, ወለዶያን for ወላድያን.

**ሰዑብ — RECOVERED (sense and morphology).** 44 occurrences across ff.160v-164r and 177v, always
paired with ርኩስ "unclean" in the anathema formula ርኩስ፡ወሰዑብ፡ውእቱ፡ዘ… f.177v found
the abstract noun **ስዕበት** paired with **ኢኮነ፡ንጹሐ** "is not clean" - same root
letters, same slot. So ሰዑብ sits in the impure/defiled field, and the formula
reads "unclean and defiled is he who…". The exact sense and etymology are still
for the expert; what is established is the semantic field, from THREE independent
pairings across nineteen folios:

  f.160v-164r  ርኩስ፡ወሰዑብ፡ውእቱ፡ዘ…      paired with "unclean" in the anathema formula
  f.177v       ስዕበት + ኢኮነ፡ንጹሐ         abstract noun paired with "is not clean"
  f.179r       ሰዑባን፡ዘእንበለ፡ይትሐፀቡ      plural + "without having washed"

The third pairing fixes it: **ritual defilement of the kind removed by
ablution**, and the **-ān plural shows an adjective or participle, not an
abstract noun**. The formula reads "unclean and defiled is he who…". Etymology
still open.

**ወቀውም — RECOVERED, and the word boundary was wrong.** Five occurrences on
f.160r, always in the anathema ርጉም፡ወውጉዝ፡ወቀውም፡ውእቱ, and listed as unplaceable.
f.184r found the **inflected plural ቀውማን** standing alone, which settles two
things: the ወ- is a conjunction, not part of the word, and **ቀውም is an adjective
or participle** — the same shape as ሰዑብ/ሰዑባን. So the formula is a THREE-term
anathema, "accursed and excommunicate and ቀውም is he who…", not two terms plus an
unknown. Sense still for the expert.

**ዕቢራት / ዕቢሬ — RECOVERED as "widows" (referent, not etymology).** Four
attestations across four folios, and the pairing settles it:

    f.174r l.19   ...ደናግል።ወዕቢራት          after "virgins"
    f.162r l.23   ...ማውታ፡ወዕቢሬ             beside "orphans", singular form
    f.175v l.4    a charity distribution list
    f.185v l.11   ለዕቢራት፡ወለእጓለ፡ማውታ        "to the ___ AND TO THE ORPHANS"

"Widows and orphans" is the fixed biblical pair, and f.185v gives it verbatim.
Two agents had independently written "slot wants widows" while refusing to
smooth the ink, since it is plainly not መበለት. **Refusing to smooth is what made
the recovery possible** — a normalised መበለታት would have erased the evidence that
this compiler uses a different word.

Etymology unknown and left to the expert; the referent is fixed.

**Vocabulary the agents could not place** - collect these for the expert rather
than guessing: ይትከነስ (recurs in the penitential canons; plausibly an Arabic
k-n-s loan, kanisa "church", so "be re-churched/re-admitted" - fits the context
exactly), ሰዑብ (always paired with ርኩስ), ወቀውም (five times in the anathema
formula), ጋልዮ/ግልዮ (a divinatory practice, possibly an Arabic loan).

## Process rules, each bought with a failed run

1. **No tooling.** No font rendering, classifiers, feature extraction or
   template matching against a modern typeface. A digital Ethiopic face is not
   a 19th-century hand and matching against one gives confident wrong readings,
   precisely on vowel order. This is what made high-effort runs never terminate.
2. **40 tool calls maximum.** Partial output beats none. Uncapped runs have hit
   119 calls, and two returned nothing at all.
3. **Unique crop filenames per folio and agent.** The Read tool has served a
   *cached image of a different page* to an agent using generic names like
   `strip4.png`. That is the mechanism by which a folio comes back transcribed
   as something that was never on it, and it is invisible in the output.
4. **Fresh context per folio.** No exceptions.
5. **Transcribe in consecutive pairs and check the seam.** See below.

## The join test — the best witness-free check available

Consecutive sides must read continuously. Three seams tested, three clean joins,
each from agents with no shared context:

    156r "...what was said in the Gospel, and from" -> 156v the fig-tree saying
    156v "the trumpet is blown three times"         -> 157r first, second, third blast
    157r "...weeping and gnashing of teeth"         -> 157v "and the outer darkness"

A model cannot fake this without having read both pages. It beats TTR, which is
genre-confounded (a liturgical page scored 0.81 legitimately), and it beats the
model's own confidence notes, which can be written fluently over fabrication.

**Strengthen it by making the prediction first.** Ask each agent to return its
first and last line verbatim and to state what the adjacent pages must contain.
f.160v did this unprompted and well: "160r should end with a word ending in ...ን
completing ንተ, and 161r should open with the object of ዘይሠርቅ (he who steals)."
A prediction registered before the neighbouring page is read is a real test; a
join noticed afterwards can be rationalised.
